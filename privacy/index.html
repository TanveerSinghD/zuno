YOUR MUSIC, YOUR CHOICE

A little clarity.
On privacy.

Updated 7 October 2026. Zuno is an iPhone app in development. This page describes the current implementation; it will be updated if the app changes.

Your library

When you connect a music service, Zuno requests permission to read song details and accessible playlists. Imported details are saved in the app’s storage on your device so your collection can be shown again when you reopen Zuno. No audio files are downloaded by the import.

Account access

Apple Music access uses Apple’s system permission flow. Spotify sign-in uses Spotify’s authorization page in a system browser session. Zuno does not ask for or store your music-service password. Spotify connection tokens are stored in the device’s Keychain and used to refresh library access.

Service requests

Library imports and refreshes request information from Apple Music or Spotify. Album artwork can be loaded from the music services’ image servers. Opening a song sends you to the original service. Those services handle these requests under their own privacy policies.

Disconnecting

The app’s library menu includes “Disconnect and clear library.” This removes Zuno’s saved library and Spotify connection tokens from the device. You can also revoke Zuno’s access through the original music service, or manage Apple Music permission in iPhone Settings.

This website

This website contains no analytics scripts, tracking cookies, account forms, or mailing-list signup. Its preview uses illustrative music titles, not a real user’s library. The hosting provider may process ordinary request data such as your IP address to serve the website. The Spotify return page does not exchange authorization codes or import music; the iPhone app handles the connection.
